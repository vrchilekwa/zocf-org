The importance of keeping in touch as a community

By Dr Lackson Mzizi · ZOCF Pastoral Department

“Tychicus, the dear brother and faithful servant in the Lord, will tell you everything, so that you also may know how I am and what I am doing. I am sending him to you for this very purpose, that you may know how we are, and that he may encourage you.”
Ephesians 6:21–22

Paul turns from teaching and encouragement to something very practical. He sends Tychicus, to keep the believers informed about his circumstances and to encourage them. The verses remind us of the importance of fellowship.

Paul understood that people need more than information, they need connection. The believers in Ephesians cared about Paul and wanted to know how he was doing. Paul took a deliberate step to communicate with them. A community requires intentional effort to keep in touch.

In our busy lives, it is easy to become isolated. We can assume that others are doing well or that they know we care about them. Christian fellowship flourishes when we actively reach out. A phone call, a text message, a visit, an email can strengthen relationships and provide encouragement. Just as Tychicus carried news from Paul, we can be channels of encouragement and connection within our communities, families, and other groups.

Communication has a purpose beyond exchanging information. Paul wanted the believers to be encouraged. When we keep in touch, our aim should be to build one another up in faith, offer support during difficulties, share joys and burdens, and remind each other of God’s faithfulness.

There is also a lesson about reliability. Paul describes Tychicus as a “dear brother and faithful servant.” Strong communities depend on faithful people who are willing to invest time and energy in maintaining relationships. We can ask ourselves: Am I someone who regularly checks on others? Do I notice when someone has been absent or struggling? Am I willing to be a source of encouragement?

Let us pray

Lord, thank You for placing us in a community of believers. Help us to be intentional in keeping in touch with one another and not to neglect the relationships You have given us. Make us faithful encouragers, like Tychicus, who bring comfort, support, and hope to others. Teach us to care for each other and to strengthen the bonds of fellowship. In Jesus’ name. Amen.

Dr Lackson Mzizi
