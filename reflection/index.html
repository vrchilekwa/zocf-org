Sunday 15 November 2026

The importance of keeping in touch as a community

By Dr Lackson Mzizi · ZOCF Pastoral Department

“Tychicus, the dear brother and faithful servant in the Lord, will tell you everything, so that you also may know how I am and what I am doing. I am sending him to you for this very purpose, that you may know how we are, and that he may encourage you.”
Ephesians 6:21–22

Paul turns from teaching and encouragement to something very practical. He sends Tychicus, to keep the believers informed about his circumstances and to encourage them. The verses remind us of the importance of fellowship.

Paul understood that people need more than information, they need connection. The believers in Ephesians cared about Paul and wanted to know how he was doing. Paul took a deliberate step to communicate with them. A community requires intentional effort to keep in touch.

In our busy lives, it is easy to become isolated. We can assume that others are doing well or that they know we care about them. Christian fellowship flourishes when we actively reach out. A phone call, a text message, a visit, an email can strengthen relationships and provide encouragement. Just as Tychicus carried news from Paul, we can be channels of encouragement and connection within our communities, families, and other groups.

Communication has a purpose beyond exchanging information. Paul wanted the believers to be encouraged. When we keep in touch, our aim should be to build one another up in faith, offer support during difficulties, share joys and burdens, and remind each other of God’s faithfulness.

There is also a lesson about reliability. Paul describes Tychicus as a “dear brother and faithful servant.” Strong communities depend on faithful people who are willing to invest time and energy in maintaining relationships. We can ask ourselves: Am I someone who regularly checks on others? Do I notice when someone has been absent or struggling? Am I willing to be a source of encouragement?

Let us pray

Lord, thank You for placing us in a community of believers. Help us to be intentional in keeping in touch with one another and not to neglect the relationships You have given us. Make us faithful encouragers, like Tychicus, who bring comfort, support, and hope to others. Teach us to care for each other and to strengthen the bonds of fellowship. In Jesus’ name. Amen.

Dr Lackson Mzizi

Sunday 8 November 2026 · Philippians 4:8 Fix your thoughts on what is true By Joane Musukwa · ZOCF Pastoral Department +
“And now, dear brothers and sisters, one final thing. Fix your thoughts on what is true, and honourable, and right, and pure, and lovely, and admirable. Think about things that are excellent and worthy of praise.”
Philippians 4:8 (NLT)

Good morning beloved of God, and greetings in Jesus’ Name.

We thank God for a brand-new week. How was your past week? We thank God for every ‘win’ gained this past week and bring our worries and anxieties to God. As we tell Him what we need, let us thank Him for all He has done.

What does this new week look like for you? Whatever that may look like, God’s word guides our thinking about our lives, our families and the world around us. Scripture encourages us to bring every concern to God in prayer and shows us where to focus our thoughts.

Practice qualifying each thought – whether it’s about life in general, people, or God.

Is what we are thinking about true? Is there any honour in that thought? Is it right for us to think like that? Is it a pure thought? Is it a lovely thought? Can we admire the thought or can other people, if they were to know our thought? Is it an excellent thought? Can it be praised as an excellent thought?

If a thought fails any of the above questions, it does not deserve our attention and focus.

This week, identify any areas where our thoughts have failed the above test. Ask God for a renewed mind.

Let us pray

Heavenly Father, we commit this new week to You. Whatever our circumstances this week, help us to choose good thoughts and focus only on what is true, honourable, right, pure, lovely, admirable, excellent and worthy of praise. In Jesus’ name we pray. Amen.

Shalom and have a blessed Sunday and week ahead.

Joane Musukwa

Sunday 1 November 2026 · Psalm 143:5 Grateful for the Past, Thankful for the Future By Pastor Simon Nkole · ZOCF Pastoral Department +
“I remember the days of old; I ponder all your great works and think about what you have done.”
Psalm 143:5

Scripture encourages us to reflect on past achievements and to look ahead with confidence and anticipation.

When we acknowledge what God has done in the past, gratitude naturally follows. Count your blessings, and remember that it is God who has helped you succeed. Take time to reflect on all that has happened in your life, and give thanks to Him.

Give thanks even for small achievements. It is easy to focus on what is wrong rather than to see the hand of God at work. As we cross into the eleventh month, let us pause and ponder all that God has done over the past ten months. We have come this far because He has been on our side.

Despite your challenges, reflect on the good things God has done. As the hymn says, “Count your blessings, name them one by one, and it will surprise you what the Lord has done.” Remember the person God brought into your life, the business deal that went through, the pay rise you received, the accident you survived, and the healing you experienced, among other things.

May this new month of November bring hope as we position ourselves for transition.

God has been good to you. Thank You, Lord. Amen.

Pastor Simon Nkole
