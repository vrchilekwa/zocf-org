Our approach

Five principles that guide our volunteering

Our Christian faith calls us to demonstrate love through practical action, recognising the dignity, gifts and needs of every person. We want volunteering to be a natural expression of community living rather than an occasional activity.

Community living

Building meaningful relationships and a sense of belonging.

Service

Using our gifts and resources for the benefit of others.

Participation

Encouraging members to become active contributors.

Stewardship

Managing entrusted resources responsibly and transparently.

Intergenerational responsibility

Preparing and empowering the next generation.

Ways to volunteer

Seven volunteering pathways

Our volunteering model accommodates members with different levels of availability, experience and personal circumstances. You do not need to make a substantial or continuous commitment to take part.

Community volunteering

Helping with events, visits, outreach and practical activities.

Skills-based volunteering

Offering professional knowledge or technical expertise.

Project volunteering

Supporting a defined initiative for an agreed period.

Mentorship

Supporting individuals or groups through guidance and experience.

Leadership volunteering

Coordinating chapters, initiatives or volunteer teams.

Digital volunteering

Supporting communications, websites, research and online resources.

Partnership volunteering

Taking part in activities organised with external charities or institutions.

Flexible participation

Give what you can

Occasionally

Help at an event, make a visit or lend a hand when you are able.

For a single project

Support one defined initiative from start to finish.

Regularly

For example, one or two days a month reviewing proposals, mentoring young people, helping with the website or supporting an initiative.

The emphasis is on meaningful contribution rather than the number of hours volunteered. No member should feel pressured to volunteer beyond their capacity.

Training and support

We equip our volunteers

Training is a central part of our approach. Depending on their responsibilities, volunteers may receive guidance in:

  • ZOCF’s vision, values and community philosophy
  • Communication and relationship building
  • Volunteer responsibilities and boundaries
  • Safeguarding and confidentiality
  • Project planning and coordination
  • Cultural awareness and intergenerational engagement
  • Basic financial accountability
  • Monitoring and reporting

Members coordinating initiatives may receive additional support in team leadership, conflict resolution, partnership development, fundraising and programme management. Volunteers are not expected to provide regulated medical, counselling or other professional services unless appropriately qualified and authorised.

The role of every member

Three questions to consider

1

What needs do I see in our community?

2

What skills, experiences or resources can I contribute?

3

Who can I work with to make a positive difference?

Not everyone will lead a chapter or manage a project. Some will contribute professional expertise; others will mentor, provide practical help, share ideas, make introductions or support a specific initiative. Every contribution has value, including the quieter contributions that sustain community life.
