Understanding ZOCF

A community built by its members

The Zambia Overseas Christian Fellowship (ZOCF) is a community-based, member-led organisation bringing together Zambian Christians living in the diaspora, particularly in the United Kingdom.

At its heart, ZOCF is about building and nurturing a thriving community in which people connect, support one another, share experiences, develop their potential and contribute positively to society.

ZOCF does not operate simply as an organisation that arranges events for its members. It is a community-building organisation whose activities, programmes and initiatives are conceived, developed and delivered through the active participation of its members.

A thriving community is built by the people who belong to it, through the relationships they develop, the responsibilities they share and the contributions they make to one another.

Our philosophy

It takes a village

Community living is the foundation of ZOCF. It recognises that human beings are created for relationships and that our development as individuals is inseparable from our relationships with others.

The African proverb, “It takes a village to raise a child,” captures this. Raising and nurturing an individual is a shared responsibility involving the wider community.

The Insaka

In Bemba tradition, the Insaka was a communal space where people gathered to exchange ideas, discuss matters affecting their community, share knowledge, resolve concerns and strengthen relationships. It represented participation, belonging, collective responsibility and mutual support.

The city gates

The biblical tradition also values communal interaction. Proverbs 31:23 describes the city gates, where community leaders and elders gathered for public discussion, community affairs, justice and decision-making.

Community participation and collective responsibility are deeply embedded in both biblical and African traditions. ZOCF seeks to bring these principles into contemporary community life.

Developing the whole person

More than career and achievement

Career progression, business, education and financial achievement are important, but they do not alone produce a well-rounded individual. People also need opportunities to develop:

  • Social confidence and interpersonal skills
  • Emotional intelligence and resilience
  • Leadership and collaborative abilities
  • Communication and relationship-building skills
  • Cultural identity and a sense of belonging
  • Spiritual maturity and moral values
  • Empathy, compassion and a commitment to serving others
  • The ability to work with people from different backgrounds

A healthy community provides an environment in which these qualities develop naturally through participation, interaction, shared experiences and service. People who develop them in a supportive environment are better equipped to take part confidently in wider society.

Our Christian foundation

The Kingdom of God

ZOCF’s understanding of community living is grounded in Christian faith, particularly the teachings of Jesus Christ concerning the Kingdom of God: not merely a theological concept, but a practical expression of God’s character through human relationships and community life.

It begins in creation

In Genesis 1:26–27, humanity is created in the image and likeness of God. Every individual therefore deserves dignity, respect, fairness and opportunities to flourish. The Kingdom is associated with righteousness, justice, equity, compassion and responsible stewardship.

Jesus demonstrated it

Jesus challenged the barriers that kept people from acceptance, dignity and belonging.

  • Children (Mark 10:13–16)
  • Women (John 8:1–11)
  • Social class: Zacchaeus (Luke 19:1–10)
  • Regional divisions: the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25–37)
  • Leaders: justice, mercy and faithfulness (Matthew 23:23)

It is a way of living

The early Church demonstrated fellowship, shared responsibility, generosity and mutual support (Acts 2:42–47). For ZOCF, community living translates Christian faith into meaningful relationships, service, justice, inclusion and responsibility towards one another.

Culture and identity

A universal message, understood through culture

God created humanity with different nationalities, languages and cultural expressions. In Acts 17, Paul addressed the people of Athens through expressions and ideas they already recognised. The message of the Kingdom is universal, but people encounter and understand its values within particular cultural contexts.

This does not mean every cultural practice is automatically consistent with Christian teaching. Many cultures, however, contain valuable expressions of wisdom, social responsibility, compassion, justice, respect and communal living. ZOCF draws from the richness of Zambian cultural knowledge to communicate values that matter across societies.

How ZOCF works

A grassroots, member-led model

ZOCF is not an organisation where a small group of leaders makes decisions and organises activities for everyone else. Members take ownership of their community by identifying needs, developing ideas, proposing initiatives and volunteering to make them happen.

Community initiatives

The activities, programmes and projects through which ZOCF delivers its vision. They emerge from the interests, ideas, needs and aspirations of members.

Steering committees

Members who volunteer some of their time, perhaps one or two days a month, to turn ideas into practical activities. They facilitate community participation; they do not replace it.

Volunteering and shared ownership

Our greatest resource is the collective knowledge, experience, skills, time and goodwill of our members. Even one day a month can make a difference.

Members are not simply beneficiaries of ZOCF; they are its builders, contributors and custodians.

What makes ZOCF different

Community ownership, not dependency

1

Member ownership

2

Distributed leadership

3

Volunteer-driven participation

Members contribute their time, experience and skills to help the community function.

4

Relationship-centred development

Activities are designed to strengthen relationships, belonging and social connections, not simply to deliver events.

5

Whole-person development

Social, emotional, spiritual, cultural and practical development alongside professional and economic advancement.

6

Cultural preservation and knowledge transfer

Traditional knowledge, language, stories and values are developed into learning resources for leaders and younger generations.

7

Intergenerational development

Older and younger generations learn from one another and maintain meaningful connections.

8

Mutual care and practical solidarity

Community support arrangements, including bereavement assistance, demonstrate collective responsibility in difficult times.

9

Wider societal contribution

The experience, relationships and capabilities developed within our community contribute positively to wider UK society.

Wider society

Our contribution beyond our membership

Many challenges facing contemporary society relate to weakening social connections, isolation and reduced community participation. Digital connectivity does not always translate into meaningful human relationships. Through its activities and partnerships, ZOCF seeks to:

  • Promote social inclusion and community integration
  • Encourage volunteering and active citizenship
  • Strengthen relationships across generations
  • Support personal development and social skills
  • Promote cultural understanding and exchange
  • Encourage charitable works and community service
  • Create opportunities for mentoring and knowledge sharing
  • Support people experiencing isolation
  • Develop partnerships with churches, charities, local authorities and community organisations
  • Preserve cultural knowledge while encouraging participation in wider society

Our vision for the future

The community we are building

  • Every member has an opportunity to participate
  • Regional chapters are active and responsive to local needs
  • Functional chapters offer meaningful opportunities for shared interests and development
  • Steering committees are supported by committed volunteers
  • Initiatives grow from the ideas and aspirations of members
  • Young people are actively engaged and supported by older generations
  • Knowledge, experience and resources are shared across the community
  • Zambian languages, heritage and traditional wisdom are preserved and passed on
  • Community leaders have access to training and development resources
  • A sustainable ZOCF training toolkit equips current and future generations
  • Charitable works and volunteering are an integral part of community life
  • Bereavement support and other mutual assistance strengthen community resilience
  • Partnerships enable projects with wider social impact
  • Our model is sustainable and can be shared with other communities
We are not simply building an organisation. We are building a community in which people can belong, grow, learn, serve, support one another and discover their purpose.
