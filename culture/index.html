The next generation

Growing up between cultures

Many children growing up in the diaspora are part of what is often called the third-culture generation. They are growing up in a society different from the one in which their parents were raised.

Although they may have a Zambian family background, their everyday experiences, education and friendships are often predominantly British. Parents may refer to sayings, practices and community values that their children have never directly experienced, and this can create a disconnect between generations.

ZOCF seeks to bridge this gap through cultural education, language training, mentorship, storytelling and structured learning resources, so that younger generations can:

  • Learn Zambian languages and cultural expressions
  • Understand their family and cultural heritage
  • Appreciate traditional wisdom and community values
  • Learn from experiences familiar to their parents and grandparents
  • Develop confidence in their cultural identity
  • Build relationships across generations
  • See how cultural values apply to today’s challenges
  • Participate confidently in wider British and international society
The objective is not simply to teach children about the past. It is to equip them with cultural understanding and personal confidence that they can use in their present and future lives.

Cultural knowledge

Amapinda: proverbs and traditional wisdom

Zambian culture contains a rich collection of proverbs, traditional sayings, stories, customs and community practices. These include Amapinda (proverbs), which communicate wisdom about relationships, responsibility, leadership, decision-making, respect, cooperation and human behaviour.

Different cultures often express similar human values through different stories and sayings. English culture draws on writers such as William Shakespeare to communicate aspects of language, behaviour and society. In the same way, ZOCF seeks to develop accessible learning materials from Zambian cultural knowledge and traditions.

These resources should not be lost or disconnected from future generations. They should be collected, interpreted, documented and developed into useful learning resources: a bridge between cultural identity and wider participation in society.

Proverbs and their meanings will be published here once they have been researched and verified.

Know a proverb, story or tradition worth recording? Email contact@zocf.org.

Leadership development

Developing community leaders

It is not enough to organise activities. We must also equip members to lead, contribute, teach, mentor and develop future generations, building leadership capacity throughout the organisation rather than depending on a small number of individuals. Training may cover:

  • Community leadership and participation
  • Organising and managing community initiatives
  • Communication and relationship-building
  • Volunteer management and coordination
  • Mentorship and intergenerational engagement
  • Conflict resolution and mediation
  • Community project development
  • Governance and accountability
  • Fundraising and resource mobilisation
  • Cultural awareness and community integration

The ZOCF training toolkit

A knowledge base to pass from one generation to the next

ZOCF is developing a structured collection of educational and training resources for community leaders, volunteers, mentors and members. The toolkit will draw on:

  • Zambian proverbs and traditional sayings
  • African communal traditions and practices
  • Biblical principles and Christian teachings
  • Community leadership experiences
  • Historical and cultural knowledge
  • Intergenerational stories and lessons
  • Practical guidance on community development
  • Examples of how cultural values apply to contemporary society

The resources will be developed into teaching materials, discussion guides, presentations, learning activities and training programmes. Cultural materials will be properly researched, their meanings verified, and resources reviewed and approved before they are shared. Where others already have relevant expertise, we will work in partnership and recommend existing resources.

Approved training and cultural resources will be published here.

The toolkit is being developed. Materials will appear as they are reviewed and approved.
